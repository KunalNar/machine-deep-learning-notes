1Vectors, norms and distances

Formula
$$\mathbf u^T\mathbf v=\sum_iu_iv_i,\qquad\|\mathbf v\|^2=\mathbf v^T\mathbf v=\sum_iv_i^2,\qquad\|\mathbf x-\mathbf y\|=\sqrt{(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)^T(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}.$$

Mahalanobis distance: $\sqrt{(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^T\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)}$, the Euclidean distance after "undoing" the covariance; it is the exponent of the Gaussian (Week 1b §3). Projection of $\mathbf x$ on a unit vector $\mathbf u$: $\mathbf u^T\mathbf x$ (Fisher, Week 2b §5).

Least squares in matrix form

For $X\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$, $\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^d$, $\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^N$: the residuals are $\mathbf v=X\mathbf w-\mathbf y$, so $\sum_i(\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf w-y_i)^2=\|X\mathbf w-\mathbf y\|^2=(X\mathbf w-\mathbf y)^T(X\mathbf w-\mathbf y)$. Expanding and setting the gradient to 0 gives the normal equations $X^TX\mathbf w=X^T\mathbf y$ (Week 2b blueprint 2).

2Matrices

  • Shapes: (rows × columns). In ML: rows = objects ($N$), columns = features ($d$). $A^T$ swaps them.
  • Product: $(R\times K)(K\times C)\to(R\times C)$; entry $(i,j)$ is row $i$ of $A$ dotted with column $j$ of $B$. In general $AB\neq BA$.
  • Transpose rules: $(A+B)^T=A^T+B^T$, $(AB)^T=B^TA^T$ (so $(X\mathbf w)^T=\mathbf w^TX^T$), and a scalar equals its transpose: $\mathbf u^T\mathbf v=\mathbf v^T\mathbf u$, e.g. $\mathbf w^TX^T\mathbf y=\mathbf y^TX\mathbf w$.
  • Identity $I$: $AI=IA=A$. Inverse: $A^{-1}A=AA^{-1}=I$, only for square, full-rank $A$. Solve $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ by multiplying from the left: $\mathbf x=A^{-1}\mathbf b$. There is no matrix division.
  • Symmetric: $A=A^T$ (covariance matrices, $X^TX$). Positive semi-definite: $\mathbf v^TA\mathbf v\ge0$ for all $\mathbf v$; every covariance matrix is.
  • Eigenvectors: $A\mathbf e=\lambda\mathbf e$. For a covariance matrix they are the axes of the Gaussian's ellipses, with the eigenvalues as the variances along them; multi-class Fisher uses the top eigenvectors of $S_W^{-1}S_B$.
  • Determinant: $\det A=0$ means singular (no inverse); $|\Sigma|$ appears in the Gaussian's normalisation.
Formula: 2 × 2 inverses
$$\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1/a&0\\0&1/b\end{bmatrix},\qquad\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\frac1{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}\quad(ad-bc\neq0).$$

3Rank and invertibility

  • Rank = the number of linearly independent columns (or rows): how many dimensions the data really spans. Height in metres and height in centimetres together still have rank 1.
  • A square $d\times d$ matrix is invertible exactly when its rank is $d$ (determinant $\neq0$).
  • $\operatorname{rank}(X^TX)=\operatorname{rank}(X)\le\min(N,d)$, so inverting $X^TX$ needs at least $N\ge d$ objects that are not all in a lower-dimensional subspace (Week 2b Ex 1.1).
  • The same reason makes a covariance estimate singular with too few objects: $N$ objects span at most $N-1$ dimensions around their mean, so a $d$-dimensional covariance needs $N>d$ (Week 1b §5).

4Gradients of vector and matrix expressions

Formula
$$\nabla_{\mathbf w}(\mathbf a^T\mathbf w)=\mathbf a,\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf w}(\mathbf w^TA\mathbf w)=(A+A^T)\mathbf w\ \overset{A\text{ symmetric}}{=}\ 2A\mathbf w,\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf w}\|X\mathbf w-\mathbf y\|^2=2X^T(X\mathbf w-\mathbf y).$$

The gradient has the shape of $\mathbf w$; that same shape rule tells you where the transposes go in backprop (code cheatsheet §3). Quotient rule for the Fisher criterion: $\nabla\frac fg=\frac{g\nabla f-f\nabla g}{g^2}$.

Formula: chain rule
$$\frac{dz}{dx}=\frac{dz}{dy}\frac{dy}{dx},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}f\big(x(t),y(t)\big)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}.$$

When a variable reaches the output along several paths, the contributions add up: the basis of backprop (Week 3b §6–7).

6Probability

Formula
$$p(x)=\sum_yp(x,y)\ \ \text{(sum rule)},\qquad p(x,y)=p(x\mid y)\,p(y)\ \ \text{(product rule)},\qquad p(y\mid x)=\frac{p(x\mid y)\,p(y)}{\sum_{y'}p(x\mid y')\,p(y')}\ \ \text{(Bayes)}.$$ $$\mathbb E[x]=\sum_xx\,p(x)\ \text{or}\int x\,p(x)\,dx,\qquad\operatorname{Var}[x]=\mathbb E[(x-\mathbb E x)^2]=\mathbb E[x^2]-(\mathbb E x)^2.$$
  • Covariance matrix: $\Sigma=\mathbb E[(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^T]$; diagonal = variances, off-diagonal = covariances. Estimated by $\frac1N\sum_i(\mathbf x_i-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})(\mathbf x_i-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})^T$ (maximum likelihood) or with $\frac1{N-1}$ (unbiased).
  • Independence: $p(x,y)=p(x)p(y)$. i.i.d. data: the likelihood of a data set is the product of the per-object likelihoods (Week 3b §3).
  • Bernoulli($p$): mean $p$, variance $p(1-p)$. Binomial($N,p$): mean $Np$, variance $Np(1-p)$, the basis of the error-estimate variance $\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)/N$ (Week 4b §4).
  • Likelihood vs probability: $p(X;\theta)$ as a function of $X$ is a distribution; as a function of $\theta$ it is the likelihood (not a distribution over $\theta$).

7The Gaussian

Formula
$$\mathcal N(x\mid\mu,\sigma^2)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}},\qquad\mathcal N(\mathbf x\mid\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)=\frac1{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\Sigma|^{1/2}}e^{-\frac12(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^T\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)}.$$

Log of the multivariate Gaussian: $-\frac12(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^T\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)-\frac12\ln|\Sigma|-\frac d2\ln2\pi$: quadratic in $\mathbf x$, which is why QDA's boundaries are quadratic and become linear when the $\Sigma$'s are equal (Week 1b §4–6). About 68% of the mass lies within $\mu\pm\sigma$, 95% within $\mu\pm1.96\sigma$. $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

8Handy facts

  • Logs: $\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$, $\ln(a/b)=\ln a-\ln b$, $\ln a^k=k\ln a$. Taking a log (or any increasing function) doesn't move an arg max; neither does multiplying by a positive constant.
  • Geometric series: $1+\rho+\dots+\rho^{t-1}=\frac{1-\rho^t}{1-\rho}$: the EWMA bias correction (Week 4a §4).
  • $(1-\frac1N)^N\to e^{-1}\approx0.368$: the fraction a bootstrap sample leaves out (Week 3a §6).
  • Sigmoid: $1-\sigma(z)=\sigma(-z)$; $\sigma(0)=\frac12$; the inverse is the log-odds $\ln\frac p{1-p}$.
  • Softmax is unchanged by adding a constant to every logit; with two classes, $\operatorname{softmax}(z_1,z_2)_1=\sigma(z_1-z_2)$.
  • Log-sum-exp: $\ln\sum_ke^{z_k}=m+\ln\sum_ke^{z_k-m}$ with $m=\max_kz_k$, computed without overflow.
  • Variance of an average of $M$ variables with variance $\sigma^2$ and pairwise correlation $\rho$: $\sigma^2\big(\rho+\frac{1-\rho}M\big)$ (bagging, Week 3a).

9Quick self-test

1. Invert $\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$.
$\frac13\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{bmatrix}$ (determinant 3).
2. Gradient of $f(\mathbf w)=\mathbf w^T\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf w-\begin{bmatrix}1&4\end{bmatrix}\mathbf w$, and where it is 0.
$\nabla f=2\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf w-\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}$. Zero at $\mathbf w=\frac12\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}=\frac1{10}\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-1&3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-0.2\\1.1\end{bmatrix}$.
3. A disease has prior 1%; a test detects it with probability 0.99 and gives a false positive with probability 0.05. $P(\text{ill}\mid\text{positive})$?
$\frac{0.99\cdot0.01}{0.99\cdot0.01+0.05\cdot0.99}=\frac{0.0099}{0.0594}=0.167$.
4. $X$ is $5\times3$ with rank 2. Is $X^TX$ invertible?
No: $X^TX$ is $3\times3$ with rank 2.
5. Derive $\sigma'(z)$.
$\sigma=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}$, so $\sigma'=e^{-z}(1+e^{-z})^{-2}=\sigma\cdot\frac{e^{-z}}{1+e^{-z}}=\sigma(1-\sigma)$.
6. Values 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9: mean, maximum-likelihood variance and unbiased variance?
Mean 5; squared deviations sum to $9+1+1+1+0+0+4+16=32$; ML variance $32/8=4$; unbiased $32/7=4.57$.
